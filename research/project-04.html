UN-Habitat–Collaborative Smart Inclusive Transition Project for Growing and Shrinking Cities

A comparative East Asian city-diagnosis project developing the Smart Inclusive Transition Index to evaluate how smart transformation and social inclusion differ between growing and shrinking cities.

Background

East Asian cities are simultaneously experiencing advanced digital transformation, population concentration, demographic aging, and urban shrinkage. Existing global smart-city indices often emphasize technology and infrastructure while inadequately addressing social inclusion, population decline, governance capacity, and local inequality.

This project develops a regionally adapted Smart Inclusive Transition Index that evaluates both smartness and inclusiveness.

Project Objectives

The project consists of three stages: developing a refined Smart Inclusive Transition indicator framework, applying the framework to growing and shrinking cities in East Asia, and developing typology-specific policy guidelines and an international diagnostic network.

The index is organized around five domains: infrastructure and essential services; effective urban management; social justice and inclusion; digital innovation; and sustainable production and resource consumption. The project ultimately aims to support cross-city comparison, public dashboards, diagnostic tools, and context-specific transition strategies.

Abstract

East Asian cities are characterized by rapid smart-city development alongside population decline, demographic aging, spatial inequality, and uneven regional growth. This project develops a Smart Inclusive Transition Index to diagnose both technological advancement and social inclusion across growing and shrinking cities.

Based on the UN-Habitat Smart Inclusive Transition framework, the study reorganizes indicators into five domains covering infrastructure, governance, social justice, digital innovation, and sustainable resource management. Representative cities from Korea, China, Japan, and Taiwan are compared using standardized quantitative indicators.

My role involved collecting, standardizing, and cleaning data for six Chinese cities and conducting Python-based comparative analysis, alongside designing the booklet illustration for the proposal. The results support the identification of city-specific strengths, deficits, and transition priorities. The project aims to produce a regionally appropriate diagnostic framework, city typologies, policy guidelines, and an open comparative platform that can later be expanded to other Asian regions.

A city cannot be considered smart solely because of technological advancement; smart transition must also improve inclusion, access, governance, and long-term urban sustainability.
