About — Environmental Design & Urban Climate

From Building Performance to the Thermal City

My interest in cities began with architecture and a focus on how spatial design can improve comfort, health, and everyday life. Building-performance simulation showed me that environmental quality can be measured through daylight, ventilation, glare, and energy use.

I later recognized that these conditions extend beyond a single building. Street geometry, surrounding structures, vegetation, materials, solar radiation, and airflow collectively shape the urban environment. Urban planning therefore became an expansion of environmental design—from improving one building to improving the environmental performance of neighborhoods and cities.

At Seoul National University, this trajectory developed into Urban Heat Island research. I now combine microclimate simulation, geospatial analysis, cost assessment, and AI-aided optimization to identify where heat-mitigation strategies work and how limited resources can most effectively improve pedestrian thermal comfort.

Environmental design and urban climate planning share the same goal: creating healthier conditions for human life.
