Urban Heat Island

Reading heat as a byproduct of redevelopment form — where floor-area and coverage ratios raise thermal risk, and where mitigation earns its cost.

A heat-risk transfer study analyzing how FAR (floor area ratio) increases under Seoul's redevelopment policies (모아타운, 재개발, 재건축) reshape surface thermal risk. Sites are clustered by morphological character using K-means (C1–C5), then modeled with XGBoost and SHAP to attribute land surface temperature outcomes to specific form variables.

Building Coverage Ratio (BCR) emerges as the dominant driver of hotspot transition, while Impervious Surface Fraction (ISF) drives mean LST elevation — read through partial dependence and accumulated local effects (PDP/ALE) to locate the thresholds where redevelopment intensity starts to cost the city in heat.

A parallel cost-effectiveness study evaluates mitigation strategies — green roofs, cool/bright roofs, permeable pavement — against their thermal comfort return, using UTCI as the governing metric across three regeneration areas.

燒却 — Burn Out Healing

Mokdong Incineration Plant Renovation, using View Points and Water

Integrated Design Studio (Architecture / Structure / MEP), 2024–2 Soongsil University · Instructor: Prof. Kim Hong-gi Team: Veca.K Architects

Incineration plants perform an environmental function cities cannot do without, yet remain socially and spatially isolated — pushed to the margins even as Seoul plans a new facility in Mapo-gu for 2026. The thesis reads this isolation alongside a second, human one: a hyper-individualized city in which a growing share of residents report burnout and depression. Both, the project argues, are problems of the boundary — and both can be addressed by the same design move, crossing perspectives.

Drawing on phenomenological theory — that multiplying the viewpoints from which someone regards a thing lengthens their gaze and deepens empathy — the design treats the incinerator's own sequence of purifying waste as a spatial metaphor for personal healing: a place where burning away is also a way of letting go.

The existing horizontal incineration process is verticalized onto its retained tall-bay structure, halving the building's footprint, while a single movement axis is threaded from the surrounding apartments and schools down to the Anyangcheon River — generating a sequence of intermediary spaces where sightlines and circulation deliberately collide.

A parallel water cycle system draws river water through the facility for first-stage purification, then reuses that water and the plant's waste heat to run a recycling workshop, a science research lab, and a greenhouse. The building is wrapped in a kinetic, triple-layer double-skin façade — its outer skin patterned after the hexagonal protein lattice of the Deinococcus radiodurans cell wall — that filters air across three layers and opens and closes to modulate light and ventilation.

Visitors move through a four-part sequence: a subterranean Water Garden for one-point reflection, a Meditation Room lit from openings to the sky, a Multiple View Gallery of interconnected bridges built for social interaction, and a Moving View ramp that carries them back out toward the river — rejoining the city, as the purified water and waste do, renewed.

CFD & Microclimate Simulation

ENVI-met and SOLWEIG runs across three Seoul regeneration sites, tracing airflow and radiant load down to the material properties of a single roof.

Microclimate simulation across Sinwol-dong, Changsin-Soongin, and Sinchon using ENVI-met for coupled airflow/thermal modeling and SOLWEIG (via UMEP) for radiant flux, evaluating green roofs, bright/cool roofs, and permeable pavement against UTCI.

Simulation credibility work runs underneath the results themselves — reconciling Korean national land cover codes against SOLWEIG's seven land-cover classes, sourcing defensible albedo values (green roof ≈ 0.196, after Gaffin et al. 2009), and diagnosing artifacts such as anomalous nighttime UTCI warming before they read as findings.

This model comparison feeds directly into a budget-constrained genetic algorithm optimization framework, pairing GA-driven strategy allocation with ENVI-met/SOLWEIG evaluation to locate where a fixed mitigation budget buys the most thermal comfort.

AI-Aided Design

Where optimization and machine learning stop supporting the research and start shaping the design decision itself.

A budget-constrained genetic algorithm searches the space of mitigation strategy allocations across three regeneration sites, evaluated against paired ENVI-met/SOLWEIG simulation — the core of an English-language paper prepared in both full (4,000-word) and abbreviated (3,000-word) form.

A separate comparative study of smart city (SIT) indicators across seven Chinese cities builds a harmonized dataset from Chinese-language statistical yearbooks, using PyMuPDF/pdftotext extraction pipelines and structured UN indicator tables — an exercise in making disparate municipal data legible to a common framework.

This thread is grounded in ongoing reading around AI-aided urban design (Quan et al.'s Smart Design framework) and case-based reasoning in GIS (Shi & Yeh), and recognized with the Best Cross-Disciplinary Integration Award at the 2nd AI4CITIES International Conference, Tongji University, 2025.
