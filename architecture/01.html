Architecture 01

Healing Fragmentation: Mokdong Incinerator Regeneration

Transforming an isolated waste infrastructure into a mediating landscape of healing, ecology, and public interaction

Individual academic project. I independently completed the concept development, site analysis, architectural and urban design, drawings, diagrams, modeling, and final visualization.

Award
Graduation Design Award, 4th Place, Soongsil University, 2024
Research Output
Presented at the 2024 Architectural Institute of Korea Spring Conference

The project addresses two forms of urban isolation: the physical separation of essential waste infrastructure from the city, and the social isolation experienced by individuals in an increasingly personalized society.

Mokdong Incinerator was interpreted as a boundary where human life, industrial machinery, and nature collide. Rather than concealing the infrastructure, the project transforms it into a mediating public space that reconnects residents, environmental processes, and industrial systems.

The design develops a phenomenological healing sequence through three spatial viewpoints — fixed, multiple, and moving. Voids, ramps, light, sound, vegetation, and changing circulation conditions gradually shift visitors from isolated observation toward embodied and shared experience.

The project preserves and reuses industrial structures while introducing healing, cultural, educational, and ecological programs, demonstrating how an essential but socially rejected facility can become a meaningful urban resource.

The associated research reviewed adaptive-reuse cases of idle industrial facilities and classified healing environments into physical, psychological, and social dimensions.

The project transforms an incinerator from an isolated machine into a public mediator connecting people, nature, and urban infrastructure.
